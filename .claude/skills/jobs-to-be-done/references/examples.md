# Examples

Concrete material for both modes. These are illustrations, not templates to clone — the point is to
recognize the *pattern*, then apply it to whatever the user brings.

## Well-formed job statements

Each names a struggle, reaches for a "better me," and implies what it replaces. The skill *delivers* in the
`When…, I want to…, so I can…` house format (see the last example); the "Free me from…" lines below are the
progress-form check that keeps the `I want to…` clause honest.

- "Free me from the stress of figuring out which products won't harm my children, so I can have more
  time to enjoy being a parent." (Klement's own, L6587 — replaces: asking friends/family/doctors/the
  internet.)
- "Take away the dread of not knowing if I'll make payroll this month, so I can run my shop like an
  owner instead of a firefighter." (Replaces: spreadsheet guesswork, calling the bookkeeper.)
- "Help me walk into the review already knowing where I stand, so I can stop bracing for surprises."
  (Replaces: chasing managers for status, re-reading old threads.)
- "When month-end is closing in and I'm dreading coming up short, I want to feel certain I can cover
  rent and payroll, so I can run my shop like an owner instead of a firefighter." (Same job as #2, in
  the `When…` phrasing — valid JTBD. Note the middle clause is *progress* ("feel certain I can cover…"),
  not an action.)

## The failure modes, with rewrites

**1. Task disguised as a job**

- Bad: "When I'm reviewing candidates, I want to filter them by experience, so I can shortlist faster."
- Why it fails: you can picture the filtering — it's an action, a feature in story clothing.
- Rewrite: "Free me from the fear of overlooking the right person in a flood of applicants, so I can
  trust the shortlist I bring to the team." (The filtering is one *solution* for this job.)

**2. Feature-as-job**

- Bad: "Customers want a real-time dashboard of their portfolio."
- Why it fails: names a solution ("things don't have jobs").
- Rewrite: "Settle my nerves about whether my money is okay right now, so I can get on with my day
  instead of refreshing and worrying." (A dashboard is one way to hire for this.)

**3. Persona anchor**

- Bad: "Busy millennial parents want quick healthy dinners."
- Why it fails: the work is being done by demographics, not situation/struggle.
- Rewrite: "Get me to a dinner I don't feel guilty about on the nights there's no time to cook, so I can
  feel like I'm taking care of my family even when I'm stretched."

**4. Typed job**

- Bad: "The functional job is to track expenses; the emotional job is to feel in control."
- Why it fails: job-typing — Klement rejects splitting one job into functional/emotional/social.
- Rewrite (one job): "Free me from the low-grade anxiety that I've lost track of where my money is going,
  so I can spend without that nagging guilt."

**5. Missing switch**

- Bad: "Help me plan my week so I can be productive." (Clean-ish, but nothing is being fired.)
- Fix: make the replaced solution visible — "...so I can stop living out of a chaotic notes app and
  scattered reminders." Now it passes the wording test.

## A worked grill (abbreviated)

> **User:** I think users want a faster way to export reports.
>
> **Skill:** That's a solution ("faster export") — let's find the job under it. First the push: when does
> the current export actually hurt? My guess from what you said: it bites when someone needs the numbers
> *right before* a meeting and the slow export makes them look unprepared. Right, or is the pain
> somewhere else?
>
> **User:** Yeah, it's always right before a client call.
>
> **Skill:** So the struggle is about walking into the call exposed, not about export speed. Pull — how
> are they better once this is solved, without naming a feature? I'd say: they walk in already confident
> the numbers are current and correct. Fair?
>
> **User:** Exactly.
>
> **Skill:** What do they do *today* to cope — what would this replace?
>
> **User:** They export the night before and hope nothing changed, or they ask an analyst to pull it.
>
> **Skill:** Good — that's the switch. Now the ladder, same struggle at three heights:
> - low: "give me a faster report export" (that's the task — reject)
> - middle: "let me walk into any client call trusting the numbers are current, so I can stop
>   pre-call scrambling"
> - high: "make me feel like the most prepared person in the room" (true, but too broad to build)
>
> I'd commit to the middle rung. Agree?
>
> **User:** Middle.
>
> **Skill:** Statement (house format): *"When I'm heading into a client call and I'm unsure the numbers are
> current, I want to walk in trusting they're right, so I can be prepared instead of hoping nothing changed
> overnight."* Progress check: *"Free me from scrambling to trust my numbers before a client call, so I can
> walk in prepared."* Replaces: night-before exports, analyst pulls. Forces — push: exposure before
> high-stakes calls; pull: walking in confident; anxiety: "is this actually live?"; inertia: the habit of
> pulling the night before. Confidence: **Directional** — built on your read, not customer interviews.
> Validation: find someone who switched from night-before exports to something live.
