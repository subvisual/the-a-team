# Theory — Jobs-as-Progress (Klement)

*why* behind a gate or an interview move.

## Definition

> "A Job to be Done is the process a consumer goes through whenever she aims to transform her
> existing life-situation into a preferred one but cannot because there are constraints that stop
> her." (L7674)

A JTBD is a **process**, and it is **designed, not discovered** — it's about a "better me" / "new
me." People have Jobs; things don't. Products are *solutions hired for* a job. (L1342)

## The nine principles (verbatim, L7699)

1. Customers don't want your product or what it does; they want help making their lives better (progress).
2. People have Jobs; things don't.
3. Competition is defined in the minds of customers, and they use progress as their criterion.
4. When customers start using a solution for a JTBD, they stop using something else.
5. Innovation opportunities exist when customers exhibit compensatory behaviors.
6. Favor progress over outcomes and goals.
7. Progress defines value; contrast reveals value.
8. Solutions for Jobs deliver value beyond the moment of use.
9. Producers, consumers, solutions, and Jobs are parts of a system that evolve markets.

## How to describe a JTBD — the format this skill outputs

Klement is explicit that there is **no single "right" template** (L6477). What matters are three tests
(L6478):

1. Does it help you and your team work together?
2. Does it describe a "better me" for the customer?
3. Does it help you avoid describing a product or how someone uses it?

His preferred form is **two parts: (1) the job, (2) when the job is done** ("new me" created) (L6604):

> "Free me from the stress I deal with when figuring out what products won't harm my children, so I
> can have more time to enjoy being a parent." (L6587)

Use the verb family that keeps the emphasis on progress: **give me / help me / make the / take away /
free me / equip me** (L6612). Wording variations are fine (reverse order; third person). How high or
low you pitch the job is a **design and business decision** (L6665) — hence the skill's altitude ladder.

**The single most important wording test** (L6630):

> "The most important test of wording a JTBD is whether it also describes the solution(s) it replaced."

Because adopting a solution means *firing* a previous one — a good job makes that switch visible.

**On templates and the "Job Story."** Klement is explicit that there is no single right format (L6477) —
what matters are the three tests, not the wording. He uses the term "Job stories" once, in the Omer case
study (L4435), for `When [situation], I want to [action], so I can [outcome]` statements pinned to specific
struggling moments — and note his examples there lean toward *actions* ("assign beds quickly", "mark it
done quickly"), exactly the task smell the disqualifier flags. He then distills a higher, purely-progress
JTBD in the `Arm me with X, so I can Y` form (L4460). Takeaway for this skill: treat **both** phrasings as
valid JTBD expressions. The `When…, I want to…` form is fine *as long as the middle clause holds progress,
not an action* — gate it identically. Do not relegate it to a lesser "downstream" artifact; that overstates
a distinction the book draws loosely. Practical default for this skill: **output in the `When…` house
format** (what Subvisual and most teams recognize), but draft in the "Free me from…" progress form first
to guarantee you're on progress, then render to the house format.

## The disqualifier test (the heart of review)

> "Does this describe an action? Can I visualize someone doing this?" — if **yes**, "you're probably
> describing a solution for a JTBD and not a JTBD itself." (L7683)

A JTBD "is not a task, activity, or has any functional characteristic" (L7688). And: avoid job **types** —
"Any attempt to [stratify jobs] will lead to logical inconsistencies and overlaps… each Job is a
combination of various emotional forces" (L7695). The functional/emotional/social typology rests on a
single unsupported line of Christensen 2003 (L7540). **"Don't depend on demographics."** (L7765)

## The four Forces of Progress

> "The forces of progress are the emotional forces that generate and shape customers' demand for a
> product." (L2922)

The customer feels all four at once. Two **generate** demand, two **reduce** it.

- **PUSH (generates).** People change only when circumstances push them to be unhappy with how things
  are. *External* pushes (outside circumstances change) and *internal* pushes (felt frustration: "I
  don't like how things are; I want a change"). (L2964)
- **PULL (generates).** "The steering wheel that directs motivation." Two pulls: the idea of a better
  life, and the preference for a particular solution. Customers must *mentally simulate* the better
  life or they won't act. (L2993)
- **ANXIETY (reduces).** *Anxiety-in-choice* — don't yet know if a product can get the job done; before
  first use; drives away first-time customers. *Anxiety-in-use* — know it works, but something makes
  them nervous; drives away repeat customers. (L3174, L3203)
- **INERTIA (reduces).** "A tendency to do nothing or to remain unchanged." Mostly habits:
  *habits-in-choice* (lock-in at the moment of decision) and *habits-in-use* (old habits pull the
  customer back over time). (L3212)

Key logic: **"There is no demand—and therefore no JTBD—unless push and pull work together."** (L3073)
**"Inertia and anxiety are your silent competitors"** — attack them like any rival (L3254). And:
**"If your product doesn't help customers make progress, price doesn't matter."** (L3122)

## Competition, done right

- **"Competition is defined in the minds of customers, and they use progress as their criterion."** (L1351)
  It's zero-sum — start one solution, stop another; one solution at a time. (L1370)
- **No "nonconsumption."** If a customer has a job, they're using *something* — including compensatory
  behavior, paying someone, DIY, or combining solutions. (L3592) "Doing nothing" / a good-enough
  non-purchase is a real competitor. (L4129)
- **Proof-of-competition test:** "you can claim that two products are competitors only if you can find a
  customer who has switched from one to the other." (L3779) — this is the default validation step for a
  hypothesis job.
- Functionally dissimilar things compete (Clarity: a conference vs. a video chat with someone admired —
  both deliver "be motivated and inspired").

## The two interpretations — why we follow Jobs-as-Progress

- **Jobs-as-Progress** (Christensen, Moesta, Klement): a *theory* (descriptive). Job = "the progress a
  person is trying to make in a particular circumstance." A switch happens to resolve a **Be-goal**
  discrepancy. Innovation often *eliminates* tasks ("Levitt stopped too soon… they want to install the
  bookshelves, not the hole"). (L7356, L7474)
- **Jobs-as-Activities** (Ulwick / ODI): an ideology + typology. Job = "a task, goal or objective,"
  decomposed via an 8-step Job Map; the activity is the unit of analysis, emotion secondary. Klement:
  this is repackaged task analysis — "why a lot of people claim JTBD is nothing new." (L7183, L7214)
- **Be > Do > Motor-control goals** (Powers). "You don't ultimately want the Do and Motor goals; you
  want the Be goals." Be goals are emotional and stable; Do/Motor are functional, transitory,
  technology-dependent. (L7079, L7109)

**This skill follows Jobs-as-Progress.** When a statement drifts toward a task/activity, that's the
Jobs-as-Activities pull — name it and climb back to progress.
